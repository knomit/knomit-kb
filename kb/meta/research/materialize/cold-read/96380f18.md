# Two cold reads of a mechanical graph-ordered export found what made it hard to read: no theme or date order, bare [n] citations resolved only at the end, 'superseded' with no diff, revision-history prose inside bodies, depth-cap stubs leaking paths, unglossed codenames

Observed 2026-10-25 (materialize research spike, first build; evidence in the git-ignored .claude/plans/materialize-spike/spike/COLD-READ-FINDINGS.md, repo-relative, not pinned). Two Haiku readers, each with no knomit access, read a deterministic, no-LLM export ('A') in which facts were ordered referrer-first by a topological sort of their kb refs. Run 1: 114 facts, about 126k tokens, agentic-engineering. Run 2: 41 facts, about 10k tokens, cyberai-kb changes of 14 days.

What the readers said made it hard to read:
- No visible order. They wanted theme sections (and date for a what's-new list); graph order interleaved matched facts with context, so trend facts were spread over the whole document.
- Bare [n] citations that resolved only in a list at the end.
- 'SUPERSEDED' markers with no statement of what changed (the reader's note says about 85% of cited targets were flagged in run 1; the spike report counts 87 of 89 pinned reads); DELETED facts shown with their full body.
- Fact bodies that carry their own revision history in prose (STALENESS, CORRECTION, 'earlier revision'), so the reader cannot tell which parts are current. This is a corpus-authoring property that the export exposes.
- 'Builds on' links placed mid-text that interrupted reading; '(not expanded: depth cap 4) kb/...' stubs that leaked raw paths; four citation formats in one document; ALL-CAPS lead-ins; unglossed codenames and acronyms (KEV, KSMBD).
- A contents list of about 3k words in run 1.

What they found useful: each fact's own claim-strength and consequence lines ('vendor claim', 'not verified', 'Does NOT mean'), and explicit scoping.

The two readers disagreed on one point: run 1's reader liked the per-fact id/type/confidence/as-of line, run 2's reader called the same metadata line noise with an unexplained confidence and an ambiguous 'as-of'.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: two single-model impressions, not graded measurements, on the FIRST export. A later, bounded export (themed groups, facts in full, out-of-set refs as one-line title stubs, 'updated since cited' only when the body differs) was the highest-scoring source in the agent question-answering test (35/40 with a Haiku reader; see the agent-qa fact), so these complaints do not show a mechanical export answers questions poorly; they describe why the first one was hard to read.
