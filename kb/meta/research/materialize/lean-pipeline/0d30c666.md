# Blind-graded, a Haiku article written in one call from the top-50 prefiltered facts was judged by an LLM grader to read better for a human than the round-4 reference articles written from 25 and 40 curated facts (reference rated comparable), with 0 inverted caveats in 141 checked negated sentences

Measured 2026-10-26 (materialize research spike, round 7; evidence in git-ignored .claude/plans/materialize-spike/round7-lean/ grades/GRADES.md, VERIFY.md and COVERAGE.md; repo-relative, not pinned). Pipeline under test: prefilter 50 candidates (art1: plain-question semantic search top 50; art2: the 50 most recent changed facts), a Haiku keep/drop judge (kept 50 and 48 of 50, so effectively no filtering), then ONE Haiku article call over the kept facts' full bodies using the round-4 'direct-v2' prompt unchanged. Comparison: round-4 Haiku direct-v2 articles over 25 (art1) and 40 (art2) curated facts. A blind LLM grader (not a human panel; 'preferred' means its judgement of what a human reader would prefer) saw the two articles per topic (P = new, Q = round 4; key hidden), drew 30 cited sentences each, and checked EVERY negated or restrictive sentence against its cited fact.

Results (P / Q). art1: 1,973 / 1,990 words, 37 of 50 / 24 of 25 facts cited, sample 28 supported + 2 partial / 29 + 1 partial, 0 unsupported or miscited; verdict 'P is preferred by a human reader; Q is comparable, not clearly worse' (P about one point better to read, Q about one point better as a reference). art2: 1,977 / 2,190 words, 38 of 48 / 31 of 40 cited, 30 supported / 27 supported + 3 partial, 0 unsupported; same verdict. Across the four articles 141 negated or restrictive sentences were checked: 0 inverted, 1 garbled (a Q sentence). Mechanical checks on the new articles: no duplicates, truncation or invalid fact ids; 3 (art1) and 2 (art2) numbers not found in the facts cited at their sentence, all year or day numbers (each does occur in some other input fact; the grader separately found that the art2 lead date 'In the two weeks to 26 October 2026' is in no source).

Where the new articles lost: the essayistic P articles dropped scope qualifiers that Q kept (art1: the over-delegation warning is stated only for Opus 4.6 and Opus 5; a Goose-specific finding generalised; the FaithJudge comparison given without its 'indicative only' caveat), and left out 13 of 50 art1 facts including practice-level rules (workflow-vs-agent, the four-stage persistent-context audit, coding agents degrading past about 400K lines); the art2 P had one unsupported framing sentence and a misattributed 'fastest' claim. In all four articles the uncited or editorial claims sat in the lead or the closing takeaway, but not only there: art2-P also had an unsupported framing paragraph and the Beam 'fastest' misattribution in its body, and art2-Q filed a resolved incident under open threats and called a forthcoming feature 'added'. Both new articles carried the security-critical qualifiers exactly (KSMBD, PHP CVE-2026-115560 not fixed by the 21 October releases, Brotli on one Node build only). The art2 new pack had no Sandworm fact (the judge dropped it) and the art1 pack held only 19 of the 25 round-3 input facts.

Cost, same run, at Haiku list price computed from measured API-equivalent tokens (CLI framing overhead subtracted): the article call cost $0.0130 (art1: 64.6k input, 13.1k output tokens) and $0.0050 (art2: 10.9k input, 7.7k output); output counts include Haiku's thinking tokens, which the CLI does not let the caller control. CLI-reported article cost was $0.0196 and $0.0062 (cache-write pricing on all input).

Consequence: a larger, only lightly curated pack written in one call read at least as well as the curated 25/40-fact reference on these two topics, but a reader should check the lead, the closing takeaway and any scope limit first, since those are where errors and dropped qualifiers were.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: not a benchmark. Two topics, one grader, one pass; 'preferred' is by about a point on a 5-point scale, and the verdict for each topic was that the reference is comparable. The two articles of a topic were written from DIFFERENT fact packs, so the comparison mixes the writing pipeline with the pack. It measures reading quality, not agent question-answering, where the agent-qa fact found a verbatim export beat LLM-written articles.
