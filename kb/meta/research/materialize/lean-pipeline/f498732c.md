# A per-fact Haiku relevance judge (title + first ~150 words, one call per candidate) kept 50/50 and 48/50 of embedding- and recency-prefiltered candidates, and 48/50 and 42/50 under a stricter prompt; it dropped a critical fact because its events were dated 2025

Measured 2026-10-26 (materialize research spike, round 7; evidence in the git-ignored .claude/future/materialize/round7-lean/ directory, JUDGE.md, judge/ and judge-strict/; repo-relative, not pinned). Two questions, 50 candidates each. art1 ('what are the latest trends in agentic development?'): the top 50 of a plain-question semantic search (limit 100, sorted by score, cosine x100 from 45.6 down to 35.0) on the agentic-engineering repo. art2 ('what's new in the last 14 days'): the 193 live changed rows since the newest commit older than 14 days, then the 50 most recent by as_of date (only the newest ~3 days of the window). Judge: claude -p --model haiku, one call per candidate, a fixed prefix (system prompt, instructions, question and a question-mode rubric, about 1.15-1.18k API tokens) then the candidate's title and first 150 words, output JSON keep/reason; one warm-up call then 6 in parallel.

Results: kept 50 of 50 (art1) and 48 of 50 (art2). A supplementary stricter prompt (told to expect to drop 40-60%) still kept 48 of 50 (art1) and 42 of 50 (art2); no article was written from it. The two art2 drops of the standard prompt: the ESET UAC-0099/Sandworm wiper fact, dropped because the excerpt dated the intrusion and wiper attack to July-September 2025, over a year before the window, with no recent publication date in the excerpt (one of art2's six critical items; the other five were kept and cited); and the Google Flow Music fact (an executive interview comment, 'opinion rather than a concrete recent event'). Latency per call mean 3.9 s (both questions), p95 8.2 s (art1) and 7.7 s (art2), 0 retries; CLI-reported cost $0.0225 and $0.0220 for the 50 calls (the CLI prices all input at the cache-write rate, see the claude -p cost gotcha).

Consequence: on a candidate set already ranked by embedding similarity or recency, this judge removed 0-16% and so did not act as a filter; do not count on it to cut a prefiltered list down. When it did drop something it judged on the dates stated in the excerpt, not on when the fact was added or reported, so an item about an older event that was newly reported was dropped as stale.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: it does not show the judge cannot discriminate. The candidate sets were not labelled for relevance, and a prefilter that already works leaves little to remove: the art2 candidates were selected by recency, which is what the question asks for. Whether the judge would reject genuinely irrelevant facts (candidates from rank 100, or from another topic) was not tested. Two questions, one prompt pair, one model.
