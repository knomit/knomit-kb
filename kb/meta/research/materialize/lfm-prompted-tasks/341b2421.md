# Prompted (not fine-tuned) LFM2.5 1.2B, 2.6B and 8.5B-MoE vs Claude Haiku on four short fact-transform tasks, blind-graded: LFM faithful on 0-8 of 48 outputs vs Haiku 44 of 48, and the small models copy few-shot example content

Measured 2026-10-26 (materialize research spike, rounds 1-2). The evidence lives in the untracked, git-ignored .claude/plans/materialize-spike/ directory of the author's checkout (round2/RESULTS.md and checks/SUMMARY.md), so it is a repo-relative location, not a pinned ref; the numbers here are the durable record. Complements kb/meta/research/ingestion/lfm-fit/e7e851aa.md, which measured FINE-TUNED 350M/1.2B readers; this is zero-training, few-shot prompting.

Setup: 15 facts (10 agentic-engineering, 5 cyberai-kb; 120 to 1500 words), 4 tasks (T1 JSON extraction, T2 per-group theme/intro/transitions on 3 groups, T3 one-liner of at most 30 words, T4 glossary), 4 models: LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct Q4_K_M, LFM2.5-2.6B Q4_K_M, lfm2.5:latest (8.5B MoE) on Ollama (temperature 0.1, num_ctx 16384, num_predict 4096, JSON-schema format; the 1.2B over /api/chat, the 2.6B and the 8.5B over /api/generate with raw:true and an empty think block pre-filled, the thinking-switch gotcha's second route), and Claude Haiku through the CLI (no JSON schema, only the instruction; all its outputs parsed). The call routes differ by model, so latencies and the 1.2B's behaviour are not like-for-like. Each prompt embedded worked few-shot gold written by hand from two extra facts. 48 outputs per model, graded blind (model identities hidden) as faithful / minor-loss / misleading / unusable with usefulness 1-5.

Overall (faithful, minor, misleading, unusable; mean usefulness): 1.2B 0/7/9/32, 1.33; 2.6B 8/21/17/2, 2.73; 8.5B-MoE 4/18/21/5, 2.29; Haiku 44/4/0/0, 4.69. Best LFM cell: T3 one-liner, 2.6B 7 of 15 faithful (usefulness 3.13) and 8.5B 4 faithful plus 10 minor-loss; Haiku 13 of 15 faithful. Worst: T1 extraction, where the 1.2B was unusable on 12 of 15 and the 8.5B misleading on 13 of 15; T2 (connective prose over a group) was unusable or misleading for every LFM except one 2.6B minor-loss. Earlier round 1 (free-form prose rewrites of whole facts, 1.2B and 8.5B, 10 each, strict manual reading by the spike's author): 17 of 20 misleading, 3 minor-loss.

Few-shot copying: outputs whose grader errors cite the worked examples (a heuristic matching the grader's wording, so a floor): 1.2B 21 of 48, 8.5B 16 of 48, 2.6B 5 of 48, Haiku 1 of 48. On T1 alone, 11 of 15 (1.2B) and 10 of 15 (8.5B) vs 1 of 15 (2.6B). Median latency per call: 1.2B 2.5 s, 8.5B 3.9 s, 2.6B 7.6 s, Haiku 4.5 s (Haiku measured under concurrency and CLI start-up).

Consequence: a prompt for a small LFM must not contain example content a copy would pass off as the answer (use zero-shot, or examples from an unrelated domain), and its output needs a faithfulness check before anyone reads it. WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: 'LFMs are useless' is false for classification-shaped jobs (see the lfm-fit fact); this measures generative rewriting of fact bodies. One grading pass, 15 items, one temperature, Q4 quantisation: treat per-cell differences of one or two outputs as noise. The 2.6B's low copy count is not evidence it follows instructions better; it was also the only model whose outputs were mostly minor-loss rather than misleading, but at usefulness 2.73 of 5.
