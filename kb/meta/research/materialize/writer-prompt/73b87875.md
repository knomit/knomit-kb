# Under the round-4 'direct-v2' one-call writer prompt, 0 of 185 and 0 of 141 checked negated sentences were inverted (three writer models), and agent question-answering over Haiku-written articles rose from 18-20/40 to 29/40; which prompt change did it was not isolated

Measured 2026-10-26 (materialize research spike, rounds 3, 4 and 7; evidence in the git-ignored .claude/plans/materialize-spike/ files round3/grades/GRADES.md, round4/writers/grades/GRADES.md, round4/writers/prompts/direct-v2.md, round4/qa/RESULTS.md and round7-lean/grades/GRADES.md; repo-relative, not pinned).

THE TWO PROMPTS. Round 3's single-call Haiku writer (W3) was told to use only the facts, copy numbers exactly, preserve conditions, scope limits and 'does NOT mean' warnings next to the claim, choose what is relevant, and cite every sentence. The round-4 'direct-v2' prompt kept those and added, together: (1) a never-omit rule for any item the facts mark urgent, critical, unpatched, actively exploited or 'patch now', which must get its own prominent paragraph with affected products, versions and the action; (2) no names, attributions, dates or causes that the facts do not state, no upgrading of hedged wording, no generalising beyond the stated scope; (3) every condition, scope limit, point-in-time qualifier, source-quality warning and 'does NOT mean' stated in the same or the next sentence as its claim, and a negation must say exactly what the fact says, not its opposite; (4) a lede that states findings, no meta-sentences, a 1,200-1,800-word target.

MEASURED 1, NEGATED SENTENCES. A blind grader checked EVERY sentence containing a negation or restriction against its cited fact. Round 4 (direct-v2 on Claude Haiku 5.5, glm-5.3-flash and deepseek-v4.1-flash, two topics, six articles): 0 inverted and 1 garbled (a misfiled AIUC-1 gap, in the glm article) of 185 checked. Round 7 (four Haiku direct-v2 articles; two are the round-4 Haiku articles re-checked, two were written from 50-fact packs): 0 inverted and 1 garbled of 141. The two checks overlap, so they are not 326 independent sentences; eight distinct articles in all.

MEASURED 2, AGENT QA. A fresh reader answered 20 questions per source (4 direct, 2 caveat, 2 synthesis, 2 unanswerable per topic, two topics). Haiku-written articles scored 18/40 (Haiku reader) and 20/40 (Sonnet reader) with the round-3 single-call prompt and 29/40 and 29/40 with direct-v2 on the same fact sets. Direct questions went from 2/16 to 9/16 and false 'not in the material' answers to answerable questions from 7 to 1 per reader. For scale, the verbatim fact export scored 35 and 34 and the multi-stage pipeline 33 and 34 (see the agent-qa fact).

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW. (a) It does NOT show that direct-v2 removed inversions that round 3's Haiku single call had: the inverted 'does not mean' sentences in round 3 were in the LFM2.5-2.6B writer's articles, several per article (see the LFM-writer fact), and direct-v2 was never run on that writer. Round 3's Haiku articles had no inversion the grader counted; their fault was lost caveats (single-call caveat retention 0.41 and 0.68 against 0.996 and 0.99 for the pipeline) and a few mis-scoped negations. What IS supported is narrower: three different writer models under direct-v2 produced no inversion in the negations checked. (b) The four changes were made together, so which one produced the QA gain is unknown. (c) 29/40 is still below the verbatim export (35/34) and the pipeline: the remaining loss is coverage. (d) 20 questions, two topics, one grader, one pass; the checks cover negations, not every sentence.
