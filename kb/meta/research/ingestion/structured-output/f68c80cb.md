# With claude -p --json-schema on Claude Haiku 5.5 whole-page extraction, recorded input tokens were 1.55x (round 5) and 1.82x (round 4) the single-turn estimate, with about 6-8 of 20 calls at 2.6-3.3x, and recorded output about 2x the visible JSON: the CLI appears to take a second internal turn on some calls, while invalid JSON fell to 0

Measured 2026-10-27 (Haiku ingestion spike, rounds 3-9; evidence in the git-ignored .claude/future/ingestion/haiku/round5/examples/COST-PER-DOC.md and round4/RESULTS.md through round9/RESULTS.md, repo-relative, not pinned).

Input: a structural single-turn estimate (prompt characters at 3.25 chars/token, calibrated on round 1, plus CLI overhead) was ~11,466 tokens per page in round 5 against a recorded mean of 17,809 (1.55x); per call the ratio was ~1.1-1.3 for most calls and ~2.6-3.3 for 6 of 20 (round 5) and about 8 of 20 (round 4). Output: visible result text / 2.75 chars per token gives ~4,509 tokens per page in round 5 vs 9,253 recorded (round 4: ~3,536 vs 6,932). Thinking tokens are not separable in these runs, so the hidden extra is retry turns and/or tool-call framing; the cause was inferred from the token pattern, not observed directly.

Benefit: without a schema, 4 of 129 main-points outputs were invalid JSON (round 3); with --json-schema every Haiku call parsed on the first pass across rounds 4-9 (the retry path was never needed). Pathological outputs still occur: one page produced 3 facts from 24,223 output tokens (round 6), and in round 9 three pages exceeded 2,500 output tokens per fact and were regenerated once; whether the structured-output path burns those tokens was not verified.

Operational consequence: CLI-measured costs for schema-forced calls overstate a single-shot call; compare costs only at equal calling paths, and guard against runaway outputs with a token cap relative to page length. This does NOT show that the Messages API's own structured output has the same overhead; that was not measured.
