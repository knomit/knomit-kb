# A fixed body lead-in 'In <subject> (<publisher>, <date>):' cut unresolved references in Haiku-written facts from 44.6% to 9.0% (seen pages) / 5.6% (unseen), beating inline anchoring (18.5% / 27.7%) and a title prefix (14.1% / 8.7%); in a question-answering test it removed most 'cannot name the subject' answers (18 of 180 to 0-3)

Measured 2026-10-27 (Haiku ingestion spike, rounds 6, 8 and 9; evidence in the git-ignored .claude/future/ingestion/haiku/round6/RESULTS.md, round8/RESULTS.md and round9/RESULTS.md, repo-relative, not pinned). Whole web pages, 20 seen + 10 unseen, cold-read Sonnet judge (see the sibling method fact in this category).

Round 6, same anchoring rules in three forms: no anchoring (v4) 44.6% seen / 56.4% unseen facts with an unresolved reference, dated 15%; A inline (subject, source and date woven into sentences) 18.5 / 27.7, dated 43.1%; B body lead-in 9.0 / 5.6, dated 75.7%, document identified 100% / 100%; C lead-in in title and body 14.1 / 8.7, and more residual unsupported sentences than A or B. The fixed slot is what forces the publisher and date in; the inline form leaves them optional. Later variants keeping the lead-in measured 10.6 / 4.2 (round 9, mean of two runs). Anchored facts were about 15-25% fewer per page and longer, and the judge flagged 41-61% as 'redundant' because the lead-in repeats the subject the title names.

Task test (rounds 8-9): Haiku answered questions from a mixed pool of title + body facts from confusable pages (two OpenAI incidents, two AWS resilience posts, ...), three repeats. Round 9, 60 questions incl. 8 traps, Opus grader: unanchored pool 168/180 correct, subject not named in 18; anchored pools 170-172/180, subject not named in 0-3; no pool gave a wrong-subject answer or answered a trap. Round 8 (22 questions, Sonnet grader) showed a larger correctness gap (51/66 vs 62/66).

What remains unresolved after the lead-in: document-coined labels and surnames ('second civilization', 'Compass', 'Patel'). Haiku glosses them in the body and then uses them bare in titles even when told to gloss every use.

What this does NOT mean: with the larger question set and Opus grading, anchoring moved correctness only 1-3 points; the stable gain is attribution (the agent can say whose campaign or incident a fact is about), not a large accuracy jump.
