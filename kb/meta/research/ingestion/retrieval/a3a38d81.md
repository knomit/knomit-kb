# Retrieved through knomit's real search, lesson-sized facts (Opus L) covered 81.8 / 88.8% of lessons questions at k=5 / 10 vs 63.2 / 78.5% for granular Haiku F facts, while detail and cross-page questions were at ceiling (97-100%) for every pool

Round 11 of the ingestion spike wrote three fact pools for the same 20 pages (Haiku F granular, 12.5 facts per page; Sonnet L, 4.65; Opus L, 6.65) through the real knomit_learn into a throwaway server and searched them with the server's own knomit_query (embeddinggemma, 5 results per page, k=5 and k=10). 60 questions (28 detail, 10 cross-page, 16 lessons, 6 traps); Sonnet answered, Opus graded; one run per cell.

Detail plus cross-page accuracy: Haiku F 98.7, Sonnet L 97.4, Opus L 100 at both k. All 6 traps refused and 0 wrong attributions in every cell. Lessons coverage (lenient) at k=5 / k=10: Haiku F 63.2 / 78.5, Sonnet L 82.1 / 85.1, Opus L 81.8 / 88.8. Paired against Haiku F: Opus L +18.6 / +10.3, Sonnet L +19.0 / +6.6. The pre-registered 'helps enough to matter' rule (+10 at the larger k) was met by Opus L only.

Consequence: granular facts answer detail and cross-page questions as well as lesson-sized ones; the gap appears only for 'what are the lessons' questions, where 5 granular results show a fragment of a page's lessons. The gap shrinks as k grows. Lesson-sized facts cost 1.3-1.6x the context tokens at the same k, so the benefit is coverage per fact, not per token; Sonnet L at k=5 (2.3k tokens) beat Haiku F at k=10 (2.5k tokens) on lessons.

What this does NOT mean: not that lesson-sized facts improve detail retrieval (that was at ceiling and cannot show a difference); not a result for other embedders, corpus sizes or answerers.

Caveats: Opus wrote the lessons key, graded, and wrote one pool (possible affinity, untested); only 16 lessons questions; one run per cell with no noise estimate; a merged ontology was used for every pool; the budget overran its $12 stop to $12.67.
