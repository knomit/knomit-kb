# Ingestion research verdict: fine-tuned LFM2.5 350M and 1.2B readers cannot write facts unattended (69-79% supported, misattribution) but fit short constrained transforms with a cheap check

Source: the ingestion research under .claude/future/ingestion/research/ (spikes 01 to 12; FINDINGS.md sections "Verdict after spike 12" and "What the LFM2.5 models are good for (take, 2026-10-03)"; spike reports 09-distillation-first-run.md, 11-onnx-path.md, 12-reader-second-iteration.md). That directory is untracked and git-ignored (.claude/* is ignored), so these are repo-relative locations on the author's checkout, not refs pinned to a commit; the numbers below are the durable record.

What was measured. The LFM2.5 350M and 1.2B were fine-tuned (LoRA) as a fact-writing reader on about 1,250 training examples, in two iterations, and run at 4-bit on ONNX Runtime. Share of written facts judged supported (iteration one then iteration two, judged on harder shipped-form output the second time): 350M 74% then 69% at 4-bit; 1.2B 86% then 79%. The second iteration did not move either model toward an unattended-writing bar. The 350M lost 11 points to 4-bit quantisation; the 1.2B's quantisation loss was not measured. Size at 4-bit: 276 MB for the 350M, 795 MB for the 1.2B. Speed at 4-bit on 4 CPU threads: under a second per chunk; with the selector choosing three chunks per document, 4.3 s per document (350M) and 10.6 s (1.2B). Training learned format and style (schema validity up to 99%, similarity to the teacher from 0.48 to 0.86) at a cost of minutes to hours on a Mac with no cloud tokens. Run-time features: log-probabilities for free, no thinking mode.

Dominant error: misattribution (right facts, wrong actor or subject, overstated quantifier) at every stage, and nothing cheap detects it. Further weaknesses: they do not judge what matters (a position prior beat them at selection); faithfulness drops on whole documents (chunks are their unit); they collapse to the majority answer on imbalanced data and copy prompt examples verbatim. Getting further would need an order of magnitude more high-quality teacher data, which spends the cloud tokens the project set out to save.

Fit table (measured in spikes 05 to 12). GOOD: minting motifs for a fact constrained to the shared vocabulary; entity extraction for facts; kind, type, topic and category suggestions from a fixed label set; query rewriting for recall and drafting a hypothetical fact to embed; fact hygiene checks (digest-style facts, overstating titles, missing consequence or misreading sections, how a source states a claim); compressing a fact to its slogan (the knomit-harden reading). POSSIBLE: same / contradicts / distinct on two facts as a review flag (a dedicated entailment model is likely better); reranking recalled facts yes or no per fact (20 facts at 0.3 s is a noticeable wait); answering a question from recalled facts for a human who sees them (expect 70 to 80% faithfulness; not for anything written back). POOR: writing facts unattended (69 to 79% supported, main claim in a quarter of documents); driving a multi-step tool loop ("small models plan badly; one structured call at most"); reading whole documents or deciding what in them matters. Practical split: the 1.2B for generative tasks, the 350M for classification-shaped ones.

Rule of use: transform or classify short text into a constrained shape, with a cheap check on the output; never the final authority on what a claim says.

Operational consequence: any proposal that asks an LFM2.5 model of 1.2B or smaller to drive a multi-step tool loop, or to write facts unattended, contradicts a measurement and must say what it changes (model size, training data volume, a detector for misattribution, or the task shape) to expect a different result.

Misreading to avoid: "small LFM models are useless for knomit" is false. The good rows are measured too, and the parts that work were kept (local fetching and conversion, the position-based selector, the dataset and labels, the ONNX route, the free failure checks). Equally, the measurement is for these two models at about 1,250 examples and two iterations; it does not rule out other models or far more teacher data.
