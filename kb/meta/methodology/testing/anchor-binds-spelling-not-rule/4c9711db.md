# A test that binds to a SPELLING, or a fixture that measures a value production never emits, passes for a reason unrelated to the rule it guards — three forms, all green on the page: the decoy word, the wrapped phrase whose repair manufactures the decoy, and the subtest name leaking into the payload

Found across PRs #188 and #190 (2026-09-14), in tests that pin PROMPT TEXT and MEASURE DELIVERED PAYLOADS rather than parsing source. This is `kb/meta/methodology/conformance-whitelist-not-blacklist/ef85a3c0.md`'s mechanism on further surfaces: that fact says a conformance check forbidding NAMED identifiers pins a spelling, not a property. The same is true of an assertion pinning a WORD of a rendered prompt, and of a fixture measuring a payload the system cannot emit.

**FORM A — the anchor matches an unrelated occurrence.** `methodology_load_test.go` pinned the distill prompt's forcing language with `require.Contains(t, content.Prompt, "Always")`. `distill_user.txt` contains "Always" TWICE: once in the methodology rule the assertion exists to guard, and once at the end of the hypothesis-handling section in the unrelated sentence "Always use a path under `<ontology-root>/meta/reasoning/` for methodology-type output". The pin was satisfied by the second whatever the first said.

Measured, not inferred: replacing the methodology rule's "Always — its title is not enough to judge whether it applies." with "Its title is not enough...", leaving the unrelated sentence standing, left one "Always" in the file and `TestRenderDistillWorkItem_HeaderAppearsWhenSectionPresent` returned ok. Two contrast mutations in the same worktree confirmed the instrument was working: renaming "Read the top-ranked candidate above:" FAILED, and removing `reflect_user.txt`'s single "Always" FAILED. So the reflect pin was sound and only the distill one was vacuous — one file having a decoy and the other not is the whole difference.

**This is a new surface for the second cause in `green-under-sabotage-has-two-causes`** — "a DIFFERENT line is quietly providing the guarantee you attributed to it". There the different line was code; here it is prose in the same asset.

**FORM B — the anchor matches nothing, and the obvious repair manufactures Form A.** The fix for Form A is to pin the full phrase, "Always — its title is not enough". In `reflect_user.txt` that phrase was split across a newline by the file's wrapping ("...its title is not\nenough"), so the pin matched zero times and the test failed. The tempting repair at that point is to SHORTEN the pin until it matches the wrapping — which reproduces Form A exactly. The correct order is to reflow the source so the phrase is contiguous, THEN pin it.

Form B is why "pin the full phrase, not the bare word" is insufficient advice on its own, and it is the part a reader will not derive: a failing pin looks like an honest red, and shortening it looks like fixing a test rather than deleting a guard.

**FORM C — the fixture measures a value production never emits.** Added from #190, and it widens the mechanism from MATCHING to MEASURING. `TestDeliveredPage_EnvelopeFitsItsReserve` is a table test whose rows were named "distill-cluster" and "distill-remainder", and it passed `tc.name` as the work item's `Type`. `Item.Type` is delivered on the page and therefore counted in the envelope, so the remainder row measured with a 17-character type where production carries the 7-character step type "distill" — exactly 10 bytes wider than anything the system can emit. Confirmed by rendering the identical prompt twice and varying only that field: 11,857 with the subtest name, 11,847 with the production type.

The direction was CONSERVATIVE, so the gate itself never under-protected. What was wrong was the NUMBER IT REPORTED: 11,857 went into the reserve constant's doc comment as the measured worst case, and that comment is what a future author trims prose against, with 441 bytes of headroom left. A fixture that measures a payload the system cannot produce reports a number about itself.

The repair was to split `stepType` from the subtest name as separate struct fields, with a comment saying why — because the tempting simplification is to merge them back.

**Every form presents as a green suite, and every form is invisible to reading.** A plausible anchor or a plausible fixture in a plausible place reads as protective — the same sentence MN13 uses about corpus-property constants. The reviewer who found Form A proposed the fix that walked into Form B; the implementer caught it. Recorded because a competent reader taking the tempting path is the evidence that the path is tempting.

**CONSEQUENCE — what to do.** Two rules, one per half of the mechanism.

When an assertion binds to text in a rendered artifact, assert the anchor occurs EXACTLY ONCE, not at least once. That catches Form A outright (a decoy makes the count 2 and reddens) and converts Form B from tempting into loud (a wrapped phrase makes the count 0 with a message that says so).

When a fixture MEASURES a delivered artifact, every field that reaches the wire must carry its production value, and test-only labels must be held in fields that do not. Naming a subtest is not a free act when the name is also passed as data.

Both are the same move as `ForModel` returning `(Thresholds, bool)` rather than a silent default, and as ef85a3c0's "read the permitted set from the DECLARATION": make the ambiguous case unrepresentable instead of relying on the author to notice it. An exactly-once helper was deliberately NOT added in #188 — it widens the change beyond the review and needs a decision about which anchors may legitimately repeat.

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — exactly-once is not right for every anchor.** Some assertions legitimately match text that repeats (a heading rendered per section, a token deliberately restated). The rule is that a pin guarding a SPECIFIC RULE must bind to something unique to that rule; it is not a blanket ban on anchors with multiple occurrences. Applying exactly-once blindly turns correct tests red.

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — a conservative measurement error is not harmless.** Form C's fixture over-measured, so the gate stayed safe and no payload was ever at risk. The defect was that the recorded figure was wrong by an amount nobody could account for, in a comment whose whole purpose is to tell the next author how much room they have. "It errs in the safe direction" justifies not treating it as urgent; it does not justify leaving the number.

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — this is not about mutation technique.** `kb/meta/methodology/testing/sabotage-must-delete-not-replace/101f516e.md` covers mutations that REPLACE rather than delete, where the mutation itself asks the wrong question. Here the mutations were correct and did their job; what was wrong was what the assertion BOUND TO or MEASURED. Anchor validity and mutation validity are separate failures and a fix for one does not address the other.

**METHOD.** Inspection accepted all three. Forms A and B were caught by mutating the anchor, keeping the decoy, and asserting the test FAILED, per `field-population-needs-its-own-test` ("only sabotage tells them apart"). Form C was caught by re-measuring independently and refusing to accept a 10-byte discrepancy from the reported figure as rounding — the gap was the evidence, and chasing it found the cause. A pin or a measuring fixture should be verified at every site it is added to, because the defect is a property of the FILE or the FIXTURE, not of the assertion: the identical pin was sound in one prompt and vacuous in another.
