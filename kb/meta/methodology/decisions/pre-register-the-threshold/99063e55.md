# When a decision waits on a measurement, FIX THE THRESHOLD BEFORE THE NUMBER LANDS — otherwise the number gets read by whoever already has a preference, which is everyone by then

A capacity constant was disputed. Rather than argue it, the decision owner wrote the rule down FIRST, verbatim and in advance: if capping costs <= 10% at a named shape on a single build, adopt the smaller value; if more, keep the larger one and document it as a deliberate trade. No post-hoc threshold adjustment. The 10% was chosen at that moment BECAUSE the competing argument was strong enough that only a substantial cost could outweigh it.

WHY IT WORKED. By the time a measurement lands, both parties have usually formed a view and can read almost any number as supporting it — a 2% cost is 'negligible, adopt', or it is 'real, and the coherence argument is only theoretical'. Pre-registration removes that degree of freedom from BOTH sides at once, including the person who commissioned the measurement.

It paid off twice in one exchange:

1. The number came in at ratio 1.00 — well inside the threshold — so the decision was made by the rule rather than by whoever argued last.
2. A CONTROL row, taken alongside, revealed a regime the rule had never considered: a mid-size host paying ~36% where the measured shape paid ~0%. Because the rule was already fixed, it was obvious that this was a NEW question rather than grounds to re-read the old one, and it got its own decision instead of quietly moving the threshold.

THE COMPANION DISCIPLINE, which is what makes it more than ceremony: a pre-registered rule governs THE QUESTION IT MEASURED and nothing else. When a measurement surfaces a case the rule did not anticipate, applying the rule mechanically to that case is exactly the motivated reading pre-registration was meant to prevent — flag it and take a new decision.

CONSEQUENCE: for any decision gated on a measurement, write down (a) the exact configuration to be measured, (b) the threshold, and (c) which way each side of it decides — before running it. Cheap, and it converts 'we measured and concluded X' from an assertion into something a later reader can audit.

RELATED FAILURES THIS GUARDS AGAINST, both real: a safety constant that was never swept and turned out to sit outside the binding path entirely, and a grounding claim assembled from two observations neither of which survived checking. Pre-registration does not catch those — sweeping the constant and checking the legs do — but it stops the LAST step, where a number is finally read to mean what someone needed it to mean.
