# Defects survive where the reviewers already AGREED — the deep catches came from outside the design conversation, not from more effort inside it

Two defects in one day's work were found, and neither came from the people doing the work.

1. The 2026-08-29 OOM's actual root cause — that `search_index.go`'s chunk constant and the embedder's batch constant were BOTH 32, so rebuild always produced exactly one maximal inference — surfaced only when the user asked an unrelated question ("why do we sort in Go rather than in SQL?"). A design review and two independent adversarial reviews had all missed it.

2. A live OOM path in the fix for that OOM — a gate that switched off at exactly the container sizes where it was needed — was found by an independent reviewer with no stake in the design. The designer and the implementer had both signed off on the rule.

The pattern is not "more review effort". Both misses sat inside the region the participants had already converged on: once a design decision is settled between two parties, neither re-examines its premises, and further effort by either goes into the parts still under debate. The unexamined region is invisible from inside precisely because agreement is what made it stop being examined.

CONSEQUENCE: for work whose failure mode is expensive, get a reader who was NOT party to the design decisions, and brief them on what was DECIDED rather than only on what was built — the settled premises are the target, not the open questions. Fresh context is the feature; a session forked from the designer's inherits the same blind spot. Note also that a peer who made the design decisions is not an independent reviewer of them, however rigorous, and saying so is not a criticism of their rigour.

COROLLARY for the person being reviewed: treat "we already agreed this" as a marker of where to look, not as settled ground. And when a non-participant asks a naive-sounding question about a settled area, that question is doing review work — answer it by checking, not by explaining.

REFINED LATER THE SAME DAY, and the refinement is sharper than this fact: the agreement that shortens review most reliably is not about the DESIGN but about the DIAGNOSIS, in the fix cycle. Agreement about what a bug was is more concentrated, more recent, and collapses review into "does this close the finding?". See kb/meta/methodology/review/fixes-are-under-reviewed/4f7028d9.md, and kb/meta/reasoning/settledness-shortens-review.md for the composition with repair-recursion — where the defect migrating outward and the scrutiny contracting inward turn out to be one loop rather than two problems. If you are choosing where to look, start there rather than here.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: not that adversarial review is ineffective. Both reviews here found real defects. It means their yield is concentrated OUTSIDE the reviewer's own prior agreements, so independence of the reviewer from the DESIGN — not merely from the code — is what determines coverage.
