# A FIX gets less scrutiny than the code it fixes — the diagnosis is already agreed by the time one is written, so review collapses into "does it close the finding?"

Across six review rounds on one change, EVERY defect after the first round was in a FIX rather than in the original code. Not near-misses — blocking ones: a live OOM window created by the fix for a previous OOM window; a safety gate that could not fire, introduced while fixing a gate that fired wrongly; two duplicated switch cases that parked thirty lines of reasoning in unreachable code; a container losing its warning because a predicate was REPLACED where it should have been generalised; and an operator-facing boundary figure that was wrong by 350 MiB across a container size people actually choose.

WHY FIXES ARE THE SOFT TARGET. By the time a fix exists, everyone has agreed what the problem was. The fix is then read against ONE question — does it close the finding — rather than as new code with its own failure modes. The reviewer who raised the finding is the worst-placed person to notice this, being invested in the closure; the author is second worst, having just been corrected and wanting to be done. So a fix is written under agreement, reviewed under agreement, and merged.

Compounding it: A FIX INHERITS THE SCOPE OF THE BUG THAT PROMPTED IT. Repeatedly the same reasoning error reappeared one step OUTSIDE the fixed case — a units error corrected at one threshold and left at another; a fraction perversity fixed on the inherited axis and reachable on the clamped axis; a predicate wired for the explicit-budget case that then excluded every container. The fix is scoped to the instance, the error is a class.

CONSEQUENCES, and they are specific:

1. REVIEW A FIX AS A NEW CHANGE, not as the closure of a finding. Ask what its own scope excludes — which sources, which classes, which thresholds it does NOT cover — rather than only whether the reported case now behaves.
2. WHOEVER REPORTED THE FINDING SHOULD NOT BE THE ONLY ONE TO VERIFY THE FIX. Their attention is on the case they named.
3. DO NOT TREAT A CLEAN ROUND AS A REASON TO STOP. Yield did not decay here: round five found a regression in the fix to round four, and round six found a wrong number in the fix to round five.
4. When a fix replaces a predicate or a rule, ask whether the right operation was REPLACEMENT or UNION/GENERALISATION. Replacement is the shape that closes one case and opens its mirror.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: not that the fixes were careless. They were careful, several carried more reasoning than the code they replaced, and each addressed its finding correctly. That is the point — competence at the named problem is exactly what the shortened review is calibrated to, and it is not what fails.
