# Recall before concluding WHY something behaves as it does — not just before designing; empirical confidence is what suppresses the impulse to check

The `/knomit-recall` triggers in CLAUDE.md are phrased around design-time moments ("before brainstorming", "before implementation requests", "picking where new code goes"). Reading that list as a PHASE rather than a HABIT is the failure mode: an agent recalls twice at the start of a session and then runs an entire multi-task execution loop — debugging, diagnosing failures, drawing conclusions — without consulting the corpus again.

THE SUPPRESSION MECHANISM, which is the part worth internalising: recall fires when you notice a gap. Measuring your way to a confident answer removes the felt gap without removing the ignorance. The moment you have empirically settled something is precisely the moment you are least likely to ask whether it was already known — and therefore the moment a documented answer is most likely to be sitting unread.

CONSEQUENCE — recall before committing to an explanation of a failure, not only before writing code. "Why is this test flaky?", "why does this 500?", "why is this number wrong?" are all `why does X work this way?` triggers. Reproducing a behaviour tells you THAT it happens; the corpus may already say WHY, and may say your reproduction supports the wrong cause.

CONSEQUENCE — dispatch instructions inherit the blind spot. A controller that stops recalling gives subagents no reason to recall either, so a whole fan-out of agents can re-derive what one fact already records. When dispatching implementers or reviewers into an area, either recall on their behalf and put the findings in the brief, or tell them to recall.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: not "query the corpus before every command". The trigger is narrow — you are about to assert a CAUSE, or about to act on a belief about why something behaves as it does. Re-running a test to see if it passes needs no recall; concluding what the failure means does.

OBSERVED INSTANCE: during the PR #62 execution loop, two `Library.sort.test.tsx` tests failed intermittently. Six test runs (isolation, stash-and-compare, three full-suite repeats) established the failure was pre-existing and not caused by the branch, and the conclusion reported was "timing-sensitive under parallel workers". kb/gotchas/web/testing/vitest-parallel-flakiness/806a17af.md already named that exact test file and IntersectionObserver, and its title states the opposite of the conclusion reached: the flakes are effect/waitFor races, NOT a parallelism bug. The empirical work did not merely duplicate an existing fact — it produced the specific misdiagnosis that fact exists to prevent.
