# Re-read a fact before claiming it has drifted — with parallel sessions on one agent branch, a peer can rewrite it between your two reads, and the pull to assert from cache is strongest in the closing summary

Observed 2026-09-17 during the PR #213 review rounds, as a self-inflicted error.

WHAT HAPPENED. A worker session read the decision fact kb/decisions/integrations/claude-code/init-merge/b9284197.md at the start of a review round, implemented against it, and at the end of the round reported to the master session that the fact had DRIFTED — that the corpus carried no record of several behaviours the round had added. Every one of them was already there. The master had amended the fact mid-round, from a different session, at a commit the worker never saw. The worker was reporting the contents of a read it had taken an hour and several commits earlier, and stating it as a current fact about the corpus.

WHY THIS SETUP MAKES IT LIKELY. Parallel named sessions (master / worker / reviewer) all write to the SAME agent branch of the same write repo. A fact is therefore not merely stale in the usual sense — outdated relative to code that moved — but can be rewritten BY A PEER WITHIN A SINGLE SESSION'S LIFETIME, between one read and the next. The knomit recall guidance already says a fact can lag HEAD and to verify load-bearing claims against the code. The concurrent-peer case is sharper and is not the same failure: the corpus itself is the thing that moved, the verification target moved with it, and nothing in the session signals that it happened.

THE RULE. Re-read a fact immediately before making a CLAIM ABOUT ITS CONTENTS to someone else — that it is missing something, contradicts something, or needs updating. Implementing from a read taken earlier is fine; asserting the corpus's current state from one is not. knomit_explain is cheap, and a claim of drift is exactly the assertion a peer will act on, by editing a fact that did not need editing.

THE GENERAL SHAPE, worth carrying beyond knomit: the strongest pull toward asserting from cache is at the END of a task, in the summary, when the work is done and the reading feels settled. That is also the moment the claim is most likely to be acted on and least likely to be checked, because it arrives as a tidy closing item rather than as a question.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: it is not an argument for re-reading every fact continuously, and it does not mean a fact read at the start of a task is unsafe to build on — the implementation in this case was correct throughout. It applies narrowly to assertions about what the corpus does or does not currently say.

DISTINCT FROM the re-anchor-refs rule (kb/meta/corpus-hygiene/fact-updates/reanchor-refs/01d50a21.md), which is about WRITING: keeping a fact's refs in step with a body you have just changed. This one is about READING: not asserting what the corpus currently says from a read you took earlier. They meet only in that both punish the careful reader.
