# The 2026-08-29 OOM: rebuild's chunk size and the embedder's batch size were BOTH 32, so every rebuild chunk became exactly one maximal session.Run

The knomit server was OOM-killed on 2026-08-29. Confirmed from the cgroup, not inferred: `memory.events` showed `oom 0, oom_kill 1` with `memory.max` unset (a global OOM, not a cgroup-limit one), `memory.peak` 14.58 GiB against 15.56 GiB MemTotal, no swap.

ROOT CAUSE — a coincidence of two constants in different packages, sized for different resources:
- `rebuildEmbeddings` chunked at `const batchSize = 32` (`internal/store/search_index.go`), a DOCUMENT COUNT bounding how many bodies are held at once.
- The embedder chunked at `const docBatchSize = 32` (`internal/embeddings/embedder.go`), the inference batch size.

Because they were EQUAL, rebuild always handed the embedder exactly one full batch, which the embedder ran as exactly ONE `session.Run` of 32 rows. On long facts that is a measured 9702 MiB peak.

The OOM was therefore rebuild's STEADY STATE whenever a chunk happened to hold long facts — not a rare pile-up, not a concurrency artifact. It was going to recur.

GENERAL LESSON, the reason this is worth keeping: a caller-side constant in an unrelated package silently carried the entire inference-memory safety story. Neither the design review nor two independent adversarial reviews found it; it surfaced only when someone asked why sorting happened in Go rather than in SQL.

FIXED by PR #175 — the embedder now bounds batches by padded tokens, so a 32-document chunk of long facts becomes 4 runs of 8. `search_index.go`'s 32 was deliberately left unchanged.
