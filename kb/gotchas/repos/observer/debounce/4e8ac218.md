# The SSE/index batcher commitObserver is a DEBOUNCE — every Notify re-arms its 1 s timer, so a steady write stream postpones the callback indefinitely; do not reuse it where a burst must go out a fixed time after its FIRST event (the push wake deliberately does not)

commitObserver (built in builder.go with a 1 s delay; feeds IndexManager().SyncLocked and the branch SSE `status` event) calls arm() on every Notify, and arm() stops the timer and starts a new one. So its callback runs 1 s after the LAST commit of a burst, and a writer that commits more often than once a second keeps pushing it out. Its other half — 'defer, never discard': a notification arriving while fn runs stays pending and re-arms when fn returns — is what guarantees the last head reaches the callback.

Consequence: when a ruling says 'batch from the first event' (F07 PR 4 D-window: the push countdown starts at the FIRST fire; later fires neither restart nor extend it), do NOT reuse commitObserver. The push wake mirrors only its one-running-one-pending half and uses a fixed countdown in the sync loop (awaitPushWindow). Emits are also NOT routed through it (TriggerEvent is published straight from the dispatcher) precisely so a steady write stream cannot starve them.

Not a bug in commitObserver: for index sync and status pings the debounce is the intended coalescing.

The user ruled the SSE debounce stays as it is (2026-09-28, verbatim: "no, SSE it's OK the way it works.").
