# Dropping tables that a migrator already recorded as created makes them stay gone — migrate-registry must clear schema_migrations, not just the tables

`migrate-registry` converts a pre-registry control.db by DROPping the legacy `lens_reads`/`lenses` and rebuilding them. Once the rebuild goes through a versioned migrator instead of a DDL constant, that sequence acquires a failure mode that did not exist before: **a migrator will not re-create what its version table says it already created.**

The reachable path. `controlUp` skips `upgradeLensSchema` for a genuinely pre-registry `lenses.write_repo` home — the re-key cannot convert that shape — but it still runs the baseline, which STAMPS the home v1 while the lens tables are still legacy. Any prior `repos.OpenRegistry` touch therefore leaves a home that is simultaneously stamped v1 and legacy-shaped. `migrate-registry` then drops `lenses`/`lens_reads`, calls the migrator, the migrator sees v1 applied and does nothing — and the lens tables are simply GONE. Symptom: `rewrite lens "<name>": no such table: lenses`.

**The fix is to drop `schema_migrations` alongside the two legacy tables.** Clearing the stamp makes the idempotent baseline re-run and recreate them; because the baseline is `IF NOT EXISTS` throughout with no INSERT/DELETE/ALTER, re-running it over a populated `repos`/`repo_origins` is a pure no-op on data.

**The general lesson, which outlives this specific tool:** anything that reaches around a migrator to drop or alter objects the migrator owns must also rewind the migrator's bookkeeping, or the two disagree about reality. Dropping the object is only half the operation.

**What this does NOT license.** The unconditional drop rewinds EVERY control migration, not just v1. That is safe only under the standing rule that up-migration bodies are re-runnable; a future non-re-runnable `000002` would break here. It also is not a general licence to clear `schema_migrations` — it is specific to a one-shot converter that rebuilds the tenant it is dropping.
