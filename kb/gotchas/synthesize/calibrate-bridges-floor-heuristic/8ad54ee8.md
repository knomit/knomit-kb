# calibrate bridges suggestFloors derives cohFloor from UNRESHAPED candidates, so it under-estimates the production floor

Found during empirical validation of the bridge-quality mechanism (2026-06-27, `calibrate bridges` against the trunk corpus, 793 facts).

`BridgeComponentReport` (what `calibrate bridges` runs) scores UNRESHAPED candidates from `enumerateBridgeCandidates` — it deliberately reports the raw token-anchored grab-bags so you can see the cohesion distribution. `suggestFloors` then derives the suggested `cohFloor` as the p50 (median) cohesion of cross-community (Sep>=2) candidates.

BUT production (`buildScoredBridges`) applies `cfg.CohFloor` AFTER `reshapeCohesiveSubset` — i.e. to the tight cohesive cross-community SUBSET, not the raw grab-bag. Reshaped subsets have much higher cohesion than the raw candidates, so the floor derived from the raw distribution UNDER-estimates the right production floor.

Concretely on trunk: suggestFloors recommended `coh-floor=0.333`, but the raw candidate cohesion is bimodal — grab-bags 0.0-0.46 (`Anthropic` 31 members coh 0.127; `security` 15 coh 0.305; `threat-intelligence` 11 coh 0.455), cohesive bridges 0.5-1.0. The shipped default `cohFloor=0.5` (from the trunk stress test) sits cleanly in that bimodal gap and is well-justified; do NOT blindly lower it to the tool's suggestion. When tuning, read the printed component table (the bimodal split) rather than trusting the single suggested number. A future improvement would be to derive the suggestion from reshaped-subset cohesion, or to clearly label it as a raw-distribution lower bound.

Validation also empirically confirmed the core mechanism on real data: cohesion bimodally discriminates grab-bags from cohesive bridges, and the highest-`sep` tokens (`Anthropic` sep=19, `security` sep=13) are GATED to Q=0 rather than surfaced as top bridges — i.e. the original breadth-rewarding `strength` bug is gone in production, not just in unit tests.
