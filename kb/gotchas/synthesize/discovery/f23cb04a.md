# Forward high-effort bridges are dominated by same-event near-duplicates BY CONSTRUCTION — cohesion and separation are computed on the SAME SIMILAR_TO graph, so no re-weighting can fix it

**THE MECHANISM.** A bridge candidate must pass three predicates simultaneously: (1) a shared literal entity/domain tag, (2) cohesion >= floor over SIMILAR_TO edges, (3) Louvain separation >= 2 — but the Louvain communities are built FROM the SIMILAR_TO graph, so predicates 2 and 3 are computed on the SAME representation. The joint region they define (mutual near-neighbours in body-embedding space that clustering nevertheless cut apart, already co-tagged) is densest in same-event near-duplicates: two write-ups of one incident share tags, are neighbours, and land in adjacent communities.

**MEASURED, twice, independently.**

- Blind-judged (2026-08 research spike): 4/12 of core's token-bridge pairs were duplicates of the same event, and 4.2% were above the dedup threshold outright.
- Earlier, on the emergent-fact-discovery branch (PR #97, HEAD 58f219f), review-high forward discovery (`buildScoredBridges`) against the ~800-fact trunk corpus produced 48 forward discover items, a large share of whose top-Q bridges were member-PAIRS that are the SAME underlying event filed in two folders. Concrete cases: the token `Jalapeño` bridging two OpenAI-Jalapeño-chip facts; `CVE-2026-5027` bridging two Langflow-CVE facts; `Celonis`/`Alex Rinke` bridging the same two Celonis facts. They rank near the top because the specificity term REWARDS the rare shared token, while there is no entailed cross-community consequence to discover — the right action on such a pair is MERGE, not synthesize.

**WHY THEY ARE NEVER COLLAPSED FIRST.** The review dedup pass (`dedupCluster`) runs WITHIN each cluster, while these duplicates live in DIFFERENT Louvain communities, so cross-community near-dups reach bridge scoring uncollapsed.

**CONSEQUENCE.** This is the structural CAUSE, not a tuning failure: any new bridge axis intended to find NOVEL connections must include a subject-disjointness gate and must NOT require members to be embedding neighbours. Adding another similarity-positive signal only deepens the duplicate selection. The earlier write-up's suggested remedy — drop bridges whose members are near-duplicate embeddings of each other, as a reshape/gate step in `buildScoredBridges`/`enumerateBridgeCandidates` — is the weaker form of the same move and is SUPERSEDED by the disjointness gate; re-weighting Q is not a remedy at all.

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN.** The gates are not wrong for their ORIGINAL purpose (finding cohesive cross-community groups). They are wrong as a NOVELTY detector. Nor does it mean the pairs are worthless output — they are correct dedup candidates, just filed under discovery.
