# The bridge gate stack selects for near-duplicates by construction — cohesion and separation are computed on the same SIMILAR_TO graph

A bridge candidate must pass three predicates simultaneously: (1) a shared literal entity/domain tag, (2) cohesion >= floor over SIMILAR_TO edges, (3) Louvain separation >= 2 — but the Louvain communities are built FROM the SIMILAR_TO graph, so predicates 2 and 3 are computed on the same representation. The joint region they define (mutual near-neighbours in body-embedding space that clustering nevertheless cut apart, already co-tagged) is densest in same-event near-duplicates: two write-ups of one incident share tags, are neighbours, and land in adjacent communities. Blind-judged measurement (2026-08 research spike): 4/12 of core's token-bridge pairs were duplicates of the same event, 4.2% above the dedup threshold outright. This is the structural CAUSE behind the earlier observation that high-effort forward bridges are dominated by near-duplicate pairs — it is not bad tuning and cannot be fixed by re-weighting. Consequence: any new bridge axis intended to find NOVEL connections must include a subject-disjointness gate and must not require members to be embedding neighbours; adding another similarity-positive signal only deepens the duplicate selection. WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: the gates are not wrong for their original purpose (cohesive cross-community groups); they are wrong as a novelty detector.
