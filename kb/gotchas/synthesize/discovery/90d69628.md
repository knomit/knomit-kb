# Discovery dedup gate (cosine 0.92) only catches verbatim dupes, not semantic restatements — novelty rests on the agent's default-skip, not the gate

Observed on the emergent-fact-discovery branch (PR #97, HEAD 58f219f) by running review-high (forward) and hypothesize-high (backward) against an isolated copy of the ~800-fact trunk corpus, then gate-testing 6 hand-drafted candidate proposals.

Gate #5 of applyDiscoveredProposals (isDuplicate → idx.Search with MinSimilarity) uses the global per-model EmbedderThresholds.Dedup = 0.92 (internal/retrieval/thresholds.go). 0.92 is a near-VERBATIM-duplicate floor. Empirically, a discovered synthesis/keystone that semantically RESTATES an existing fact reaches only ~0.65–0.78 raw cosine to its nearest live fact, because the reworded synthesis reframes the wording. Proof: a backward keystone deliberately drafted to restate the existing fact kb/technology/security/synthesis/e6f70a4f.md ("AI capabilities are neutral amplifiers...") landed at ~0.70 cosine and passed the dedup gate cleanly; all 6 test proposals passed every gate.

Consequence: the embedding dedup gate only stops verbatim copies. The "not already in the corpus" novelty condition is enforced almost entirely by the connected agent's DEFAULT-TO-NO judgment in the discover prompt, NOT by the gate chain. This sharpens the model-less-verification decision: the gate chain is a guardrail against malformed/low-confidence/verbatim output, not a novelty filter.

RECOMMENDED FOLLOW-UP: introduce a discovery-specific dedup threshold (~0.82–0.85) distinct from the verbatim-dedup 0.92, OR add an entailment / 'already-implied' check, since cosine alone cannot distinguish a novel cross-domain synthesis from a reworded restatement of an existing fact.
