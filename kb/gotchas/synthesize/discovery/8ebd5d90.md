# Forward high-effort bridges are dominated by same-event near-duplicate member pairs — dedup candidates, not discovery seeds

Observed on the emergent-fact-discovery branch (PR #97, HEAD 58f219f) running review-high forward discovery (buildScoredBridges) against the ~800-fact trunk corpus: of 48 forward discover items, a large share of the top-Q bridges are member-PAIRS that are actually the SAME underlying event filed in two folders (near-duplicate embeddings that happen to share a rare token). Examples seen: token "Jalapeño" bridging two OpenAI-Jalapeño-chip facts; "CVE-2026-5027" bridging two Langflow-CVE facts; "Celonis"/"Alex Rinke" bridging the same two Celonis facts.

They rank near the top because the specificity term rewards the rare shared token, but there is no entailed cross-community consequence to discover — the right action is MERGE (dedup), not synthesize. They slip through because the review dedup pass (dedupCluster) runs WITHIN each cluster, while these duplicates live in DIFFERENT Louvain communities, so cross-community near-dups are never collapsed before bridge scoring.

RECOMMENDED FOLLOW-UP: before Q-scoring (or as a reshape/gate step in buildScoredBridges / enumerateBridgeCandidates), drop bridges whose members are near-duplicate embeddings of each other.
