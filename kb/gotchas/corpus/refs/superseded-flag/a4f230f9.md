# A cited fact whose commit differs from HEAD is NOT necessarily changed: in a measured sample 67 of 87 'superseded' ref targets had byte-identical bodies, so compare bodies, not commits

Measured 2026-10-25/26 in the materialize research spike on the agentic-engineering knowledge base (agent branch of one developer's checkout). The spike read each cited fact at the commit its citing fact pointed at, then compared that with the same fact at branch HEAD. A 'superseded' flag defined as 'the pinned target's commit / as_of differs from HEAD' fired on 87 targets. Comparing the actual bodies of those 87 (token-level difflib ratio over words, punctuation and whitespace; thresholds are the spike's own: trivial >= 0.95, minor 0.85-0.95, moderate 0.70-0.85, substantial < 0.70):

- identical body: 67 (of those, 52 had no frontmatter change either; 13 changed only motifs; 1 confidence and motifs; 1 refs)
- trivial 2, minor 3, moderate 8, substantial 7.

So 67 of 87 (77%) 'superseded' targets had the exact same body; only 15 of 87 changed by more than the minor band. Why a commit differs without a body change: motif backfills and confidence edits touch frontmatter only, and the per-fact commit list returned by the branch also contains PR merge commits, so 'commits touching this fact' overcounts content revisions.

Consequence: any feature that tells a reader a cited fact was 'updated since cited' (a banner, a diff offer, a re-read prompt) must decide it from a body comparison (and from frontmatter fields it chooses to care about), never from commit or as_of inequality; the commit test would flag roughly three quarters of the targets that have not changed in substance.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: pinned and current versions are not interchangeable. 15 of the 87 had real body changes (7 substantial), and one hand-checked pair really differed. The sample is one KB on one branch, taken at one time; the ratio of content-identical to changed targets will differ elsewhere. The thresholds are the spike's own, not a knomit definition.
