# At a ~360px fact pane the header band's content is ~540px wide — the date overlaps the motif cell, the last motif cell runs past the pane edge, and a scroll-into-view scrolls the overflow:hidden pane wrapper sideways

Observed in the browser on 2026-09-27 during PR #328's check (dev at 2ed1de35 plus the clamp branch; the band itself is untouched by that PR). Setup: 900px viewport with the list rail dragged to its max (540px), leaving a 360px fact pane.

- `fact-band` measured scrollWidth 540 inside a 360px box. The header rows do not wrap or shrink to the pane.
- The date ('8/27/2026') is drawn over the 'same motif' cell.
- A motif cell's rect ran to x≈900+, i.e. past the pane's right edge.
- The fact view's `overflow:hidden` wrapper (the one carrying `data-panel-bounds`) is still programmatically scrollable. Playwright's click scrolls its target into view, which set the wrapper's scrollLeft to 29 and shifted the whole fact view 29px left, so the pane's left content was clipped. Focusing the cell alone did not scroll it.

**Consequence:** in narrow panes the header's own layout is broken independently of the hanging panels. Any scroll-into-view of a header control (a test click, a future focus management call, `scrollIntoView`) can leave the pane scrolled sideways with no scrollbar to undo it. Browser checks of narrow layouts should open panels with a dispatched click, or reset `scrollLeft` first, or the measurements describe the scrolled state.

**What this does NOT mean:** not that a real mouse click on the visible part of a cell scrolls the pane. That was not observed and is not claimed. Not caused by the panel clamp: the band's overflow is present with every panel closed.
