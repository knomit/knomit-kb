# Under embeddinggemma, K creates the SIMILAR_TO edge set, not the cosine floor — 99.6% of fact pairs clear the 0.18 floor

SIMILAR_TO edges are each fact's top-K=10 nearest neighbours filtered by the active model's SimilarTo floor. On embeddinggemma the calibrated floor is 0.18, and measured on the knomit-kb corpus (461 facts) 99.6% of ALL fact pairs exceed it (mean pairwise cosine 0.385). The floor therefore barely binds; the edge set is created by K. 'Not similar' in this system means 'not in each other's top-10', NOT 'below a cosine threshold'. Consequence: any analysis, test, or design that reasons about edge existence via a cosine cutoff is wrong under embeddinggemma — reconstruct the top-K neighbourhood instead (this exact mistake invalidated early measurements in the 2026-08 bridging research spike and had to be corrected). WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: the floor is not useless — it still drops the ~0.4% anti-correlated tail — but it is not what shapes the graph.
