# EmbeddingGemma's cosine distribution runs much cooler than nomic's; 5 of the 6 retrieval thresholds were ported DOWN (Dedup was re-derived by #638)

Measured against the real corpus (712 facts from knomit.db + knomit-kb.db) with tools/calibrate (PR #72). EmbeddingGemma produces SUBSTANTIALLY lower cosine similarities than nomic-v1.5 on the same texts:

- distinct same-category doc pairs: nomic mean 0.745 / p99 0.903  vs  gemma mean 0.483 / p99 0.766
- all doc pairs: nomic mean 0.644  vs  gemma mean 0.267
- query->doc pairs: nomic mean 0.568  vs  gemma mean 0.204
- true near-dup pairs (synthetic): both ~0.98 (near-identical text scores high under either model)

CONSEQUENCE: this REVERSES the naive worry that gemma would over-merge at the old 0.92 dedup threshold. The opposite is true. 0.92 sits at gemma's p99.6 of distinct pairs, so under gemma it would almost NEVER fire: silent under-merge and duplicate accumulation, not over-merge. Every absolute cosine cutoff had to move DOWN.

METHOD: distribution-preserving port. For each nomic threshold T on its governing distribution D, find p=CDF_nomic_D(T); the gemma value is the p-quantile of gemma's D. Ported values (nomic -> gemma):
- dedup 0.92->0.82 (HISTORICAL: replaced by #638, see the UPDATE at the end)
- reflect-novelty 0.85->0.69
- SIMILAR_TO 0.60->0.18
- search recall floor 0.40->~0 (clamped to 0.05)
- rerank-high 0.70->0.43
- rerank-low 0.50->0.10

Dedup 0.82 was validated inside the safety gap [distinct p99 0.766, true near-dup p05 0.959]. Dedup and ReflectNovelty are ported on docDocSame, SimilarTo on docDocAll, and SearchFloor and the rerank tiers on queryDoc. Only thresholds from the same distribution may bound one interval (see decisions/mcp/learn/same-subject-band-endpoints).

To recalibrate for a new model, run the `embeddings` subcommand with double-dash flags. pflag reads a single-dash `-cache` as shorthand `-c` and fails. The native libs are under dist/<os>-<arch>/lib:
`ORT_LIB_PATH=dist/darwin-arm64/lib/libonnxruntime.dylib DYLD_LIBRARY_PATH=dist/darwin-arm64/lib go run ./tools/calibrate embeddings --cache ~/.knomit/models ~/.knomit/repos/*.db`
It opens the DBs read-only (immutable=1) and prints the distribution-preserving port table.

The values live as `params.Thresholds` entries in the per-model `modelThresholds` map in internal/domain/modelparams/thresholds.go. `embeddings.Model.Thresholds` is filled from that map when the descriptor is initialized, and a model with no entry panics there. New values are edited in the map, not on the descriptor. tools/calibrate/README.md documents the full procedure.

UPDATE (#638, merge 231e5711): Dedup is no longer a port. On 1,647 facts the corpus holds sibling families and the ported 0.82 stopped sitting in a gap (distinct same-directory p99 0.874; 21 pairs in learn's merge scope above 0.82; the W0g2 decision merged over its sibling W0g1 at 0.8616). Dedup was re-derived as the midpoint between the highest genuinely distinct merge-scope pair and the p01 of synthetic true near-duplicates, floored to 0.005: gemma 0.905, nomic 0.95. The other five thresholds are still the distribution-preserving port above. The claim that Dedup 0.82 sits inside a safety gap [0.766, 0.959] no longer holds. The re-derived values are this corpus's gap, measured once, not model constants; the method is in the comment in thresholds.go. See incidents/mcp/learn/dedup-merge-overwrote-sibling-decision.
