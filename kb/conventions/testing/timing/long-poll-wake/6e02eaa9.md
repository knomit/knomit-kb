# A long-poll "wakes on the event" test (oauth /wait) uses an hour-long window and a 30 s hang detector, and waits for the waiter to register instead of sleeping (#659)

/wait re-reads the request after its window, so a poll that MISSED the wake still answers 302 when the window ends: the status code alone cannot tell "woke on the decision" from "waited out the window". The old tests told them apart with a latency bound (1.5 s against a 2 s window, and 2 s against a 5 s window). Now the window is an hour (Issuer waitTimeout), and waitAnswered fails at 30 s, so missing the wake is red and slowness is not. The approval waits until the poll has registered its waiter (waiterEntries) rather than for a 200 ms sleep. Sabotage: Approve without notifying the waiters is red at the 30 s detector.

What this does NOT mean: the production window is unchanged (30 s by default, issuer.go NewIssuer).
