# A lint baseline taken with `git checkout <base> -- <dir>` is wrong on any branch that ADDS files, and a baseline against your own branch's first commit is parity with yourself

`git checkout <base> -- <dir>` does not DELETE files absent from the base, so a branch's new files stay on disk and are counted into the "baseline" — hiding exactly the findings the branch introduces. Measured: it returned 94 where the true base was 93. Use a clean `git worktree` at the base.

AND MEASURE AGAINST THE BASE, not against a commit inside the PR. One report compared the head to the PR's own first commit and declared parity while the PR added three problems: the first commit already carried two of them, so they were in "the baseline" by construction.

Compare the `(file, rule)` MULTISET, not the total — a total hides one finding removed and another added.

AND FIX RATHER THAN ABSORB. Of three findings added across this stack, two were real (`react-hooks/set-state-in-effect` on a loader called from an effect body, `react-hooks/purity` from `Date.now()` during render) and one was a genuine false positive suppressed with its reason on the line. A baseline bump would have recorded all three as background noise.
