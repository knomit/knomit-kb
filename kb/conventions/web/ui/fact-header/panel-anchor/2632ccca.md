# Both fact-chrome panels (ConnectionsPanel, MotifPanel) are position:absolute, top:100%, LEFT:0 on the edgesGroup span — they open rightward into the pane, and a right anchor there hangs them off the pane's left edge where overflow:hidden clips them

The two panels the fact header's edges row opens — ConnectionsPanel ('cited by' / 'cites', one panel whose direction switches) and MotifPanel ('same motif' cells and the +N overflow) — are both `position:absolute; top:100%; left:0` (+ marginTop 6). Their containing block is the edgesGroup span in RightPanel (`position:relative`), which wraps the WHOLE edges row: the in/out cells and every motif cell. That span starts at the fact pane's left padding (28px).

**Consequence:** a panel anchored `right:0` on that span opens LEFTWARD by (panel width − span width). ConnectionsPanel is 360px wide and MotifPanel 420px, both wider than a typical row, so a right anchor puts the panel's left part outside the fact pane. The fact view's ancestors are `overflow:hidden` (RightPanel's fact-view root and its body wrapper), so that part is CLIPPED at the pane edge. It looks as if the list rail covers it, but it is not a z-index/stacking problem, and raising zIndex does nothing.

**The contract is pinned by tests** ('hangs from the menu, left-aligned' in both ConnectionsPanel.test.tsx and MotifPanel.test.tsx): `left` is '0px' and `right` is unset. jsdom cannot see clipping, so the anchor itself is what is asserted.

**What this does NOT mean:**
- *Not* that the panels are portalled or position:fixed. They are in-tree absolute children of the edgesGroup span, which is what lets `menuRef`/hover keep-open and `useDismiss` treat trigger and panel as one region. A portal was considered in PR #323 and not chosen.
- *Not* that they are safe in any pane width. Left-anchored, they need roughly width + 28px of pane, or the RIGHT side clips on the same overflow:hidden. No clamp exists.
- *Not* that a new panel added to the row may pick its own anchor. It hangs from the same span, so it inherits the same rule.
