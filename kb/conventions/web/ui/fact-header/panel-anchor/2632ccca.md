# Both fact-chrome panels (ConnectionsPanel 360px, MotifPanel 420px) are position:absolute, top:100%, LEFT:0 on the edgesGroup span. They open rightward into the pane; a right anchor there hangs them off the pane's left edge, where overflow:hidden clips them

The fact header's edges row opens two panels:
- ConnectionsPanel, for 'cited by' / 'cites' (one panel whose direction switches), `CONNECTIONS_PANEL_WIDTH` = 360.
- MotifPanel, for the 'same motif' cells and the +N overflow, `MOTIF_PANEL_WIDTH` = 420.

They share an anchor and a surface style but NOT a width. Both are `position:absolute; top:100%; left:0` (+ marginTop `PANEL_GAP` = 6). Their containing block is the edgesGroup span in RightPanel (`position:relative`). That span wraps the WHOLE edges row, the in/out cells and every motif cell, and starts at the fact pane's left padding (28px). FactBand draws the row above the fact body's scroll container, so an open panel stays put while the body scrolls beneath it.

**Consequence:** a panel anchored `right:0` on that span opens LEFTWARD by (panel width − span width). Both panels are wider than a typical row, so a right anchor puts the panel's left part outside the fact pane. The fact view's ancestors are `overflow:hidden` (RightPanel's fact-view root and its body wrapper), so that part is CLIPPED at the pane edge. It looks like the list rail covers it, but this is not a z-index/stacking problem, and raising zIndex does nothing. The panel-clamp only shrinks from the right and bottom, so it cannot rescue a panel whose left edge is already outside the pane.

**Tests pin the contract.** ConnectionsPanel.test.tsx and MotifPanel.test.tsx each have 'hangs from the menu, left-aligned', which asserts `left` is '0px' and `right` is unset. jsdom cannot see clipping, so the tests assert the anchor itself.

**Right and bottom (PR #328):** the panels are clamped to the fact pane by `usePanelClamp` against the `[data-panel-bounds]` wrapper. In a narrow or short pane they shrink and their list scrolls, instead of being cut off. See kb/conventions/web/ui/fact-header/panel-clamp/c0137d49.md for the mechanism, and kb/decisions/web/fact-header/panel-clamp-in-tree/55dc25ca.md for why the panels stay in-tree instead of being portalled.

**What this does NOT mean:**
- *Not* that the anchor can be changed now that a clamp exists. The clamp caps width and height from the anchor outward, so a right anchor would still clip on the left.
- *Not* that a new panel added to the row may pick its own anchor. It hangs from the same span, so it inherits the same rule, and it should also take the clamp.
- *Not* that the two panels share one shell component. Each inlines its own style literal, and only the tests keep the anchors in step.
