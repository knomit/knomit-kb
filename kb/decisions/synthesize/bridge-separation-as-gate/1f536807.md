# Treat Louvain separation as a gate (sep>=2), never a ranking reward

Core decision in the bridge-quality scorer, correcting the original breadth-rewarding bug.

**Options considered:** (a) keep separation (number of distinct communities a bridge's members span) as a positive ranking term, as the old `strength = (1/freq)·communities·members` formula did; (b) demote separation to a hard constraint only.

**Rationale:** rewarding raw separation IS the original bug — it made broad grab-bag tags win (trunk's `ai` tag spanned 36 of 37 clusters precisely because the formula paid for spread). A good bridge is small and cohesive yet crosses a latent community boundary; the boundary-crossing is a *requirement*, not something to maximize. Maximizing it reproduces the grab-bag failure.

**The choice:** `sep` is a gate only. `bridgeQ` rejects any candidate with `sep < 2` (alongside coh>=cohFloor and members<=maxMembers) and the Q score is `w_coh·coh + w_gap·gap + w_spec·spec` with NO separation term. Verified in code: `BridgeComponents.Sep` is referenced solely in the gate, never in the weighted sum.
