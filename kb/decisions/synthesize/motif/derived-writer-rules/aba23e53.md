# Derived-writer rules for motifs (MN11): mechanical merge unions winner-first and trims at the cap; LLM writers carry over; nothing mechanically stamps seed motifs onto discovered facts

Every path that writes a fact it did not receive from a human obeys these, and they are deliberately different for mechanical and LLM writers.

**Mechanical (dedup-merge).** `fact.MergeMotifs(winner, loser)` unions WINNER-FIRST and trims the tail to `MaxMotifs` = 3.

**Why order is semantic here when it is cosmetic next door — the reason to state whenever citing this.** The domain and entity unions at the same call sites are INCOMING-first and uncapped: every value survives whichever way round they go, so their order is decoration. Motifs are CAPPED, so order decides what SURVIVES. Handing the loser's naming of the regularity priority because it happened to arrive in a particular argument slot would contradict the tiebreak philosophy every other merged field follows — the winner contributes the identity. Swapping the operands drops the winner's whole axis. Bound by `TestMotifDynamics_ReviewMergeTrimsToTheCapWinnerFirst` (the overflow case) alongside the pre-existing ordering test.

**Two behaviours consumers must not be surprised by.**
- **Trimming happens in the merge, not at serialize.** Letting `SerializeFact` reject an over-cap list would fail the whole operation that triggered it — a learn call, or a review session's consolidation. A routine merge must not be able to do that.
- **Cross-parent phrasing duplicates that are not STRING-equal ride along and cost a slot** until alias resolution collapses them in derived state. That is the accepted price of resolving this mechanically at merge time — so a merge can spend two of three slots on two spellings of one regularity.
- Returns nil when both sides are empty, so a merge of two motif-less facts stays motif-less rather than becoming an EMPTY list — which matters because nil and empty must not produce different blob hashes.

**One definition, two callers, and that is deliberate:** learn-time dedup (`internal/mcp/learn.go`) and review-session consolidation (`internal/synthesize/dedup.go`). They are the same operation in two places, and the second silently dropped the loser's motifs until a review caught it.

**LLM writers (prune / distill / discover).** Output schemas gain `motifs` with a carry-over instruction; `factForLLM` shows members' motifs; all output flows through `SerializeFact`, so MN4's single validation entry point covers them with no per-path code.

**Two deliberate NOTs.** (1) Keystones stay motif-less via the ORDINARY subject strip — no strip exemption. (2) There is NO mechanical stamping of seed motifs onto discovered facts.

**Consequence for a new write path.** The mechanical rule and the LLM rule are not interchangeable: a new mechanical path must union-and-trim explicitly (nothing infers it); a new LLM path needs only the schema field.

**Caught before it shipped:** backfill originally parsed a fact's INDEXED BODY rather than its source, so writing it back would have replaced real facts with husks carrying only the new motifs — and the damage would have looked like success.
