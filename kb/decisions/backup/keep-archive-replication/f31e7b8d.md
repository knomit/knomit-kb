# Archive replication is KEPT deliberately — it is durability for cold data, and is the one part of backup that is not about cold-boot speed

Archived repo databases ARE replicated, under a separate `archive/<ksuid>` prefix with snapshot retention DISABLED (an archived database stops changing, so ordinary retention would expire it and turn 'archive' into 'delete on a delay'). The supporting surface — TrackArchived, UntrackArchived, RestoreArchived, DeleteArchivedReplica, lazy fetch on unarchive (fetchArchivedDB), re-attachment at boot (ArchivedDBPaths), and explicit replica deletion on purge — stays.

This is a deliberate EXCEPTION to [[warm-start-cache-not-durability]], and reading the two together without this fact makes the codebase look inconsistent. It is not.

OPTIONS CONSIDERED:
(a) Archives are local-only — drop archive replication AND stop persisting archived rows to control.db. Consistent with 'backup exists only to speed cold boot'; archives would not survive a container replacement, and nothing would claim they do.
(b) Archives are durable — keep archive replication as built.
(c) Drop the replication but keep the archived registry row, and make unarchive fail with a message naming why.

CHOSE (b), by the project owner, against the assistant's recommendation of (a).

WHY (a) AND (c) LOSE: the archived registry row lives in control.db, which IS replicated. On a fresh container control.db returns listing archives whose .db files are not on the volume. Archive replication is the ONLY thing that makes those rows redeemable. Under (a) the row has to be dropped too, which makes archiving non-durable; under (c) the registry advertises archives it cannot produce. (b) is the only option where an archived repo survives a container replacement.

CONSEQUENCES a consumer must act on:
- Do NOT delete archive replication on the grounds that 'backup is only a warm-start cache'. That reasoning is correct for LIVE repos and does not extend here — archived repos are not served and never participate in a boot, so the justification for replicating them is durability, full stop.
- The archive prefix accumulates objects FOREVER. Retention is off by design, so Purge's DeleteArchivedReplica is the only thing that ever reclaims them. A purge path that skips it is a storage leak, not a cosmetic bug.

NON-SCOPE: this does not reopen any of the boot-path refusals removed in [[warm-start-cache-not-durability]]. A failure to replicate or restore an ARCHIVE must still never refuse a boot; archives are cold data and are fetched lazily on unarchive.
