# Reader priors are learned hybrid: passive usage/effect telemetry by default, targeted probes for high-value facts

Decided 2026-07-04 during the stochastic-target roadmap rethink; third decision in the sequence (after two-layer fact value and belief+posterior).

Context: the emit layer (librarian) needs prior-delta per (fact, reader model) — what the reader already believes — to decide what is worth emitting (delta ≈ 0 → dead weight), what to emit normally, and what must be emitted with extra evidentiary force (the reader confidently believes the opposite).

Options considered:
1. Active probing as the foundation — blind-query the reader per fact, cached per model version. Rejected as the default: eval cost per fact per reader, probes go stale on every model release.
2. Passive only — profiles from usage telemetry + injection/ablation effects. Rejected as sole mechanism: slow to converge, coverage-skewed to hot areas, and weak at separating 'reader already knew this' from 'fact was irrelevant to the task' (opposite signals for the librarian).
3. Hybrid (CHOSEN): passive telemetry builds profiles by default (rides real sessions, free-ish); probe budget is spent only where it pays — context-pack member facts, high-posterior high-blast facts, and a re-probe sweep on model succession. Probe priority is itself learned from effect data.

Note: probe outcomes distinguish three states per (fact, reader): already-known (suppress emission), unknown (emit normally), confidently-wrong prior (emit with force and receipts) — the third is the librarian's highest-value case.
