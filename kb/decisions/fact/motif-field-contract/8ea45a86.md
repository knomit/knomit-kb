# The motif field contract: validated in SerializeFact ONLY, dropped rather than rejected at parse — so a version legal when committed stays readable forever

`motifs:` on `fact.Fact`, in frontmatter and JSON. A motif is a 2–4 word kebab-case noun phrase; at most `MaxMotifs` = 3 per fact.

**Where validation lives, and why only there.** The rules are applied in `SerializeFact` and nowhere else — the a63ff254 single-entry-point pattern. That is what makes every write path (learn, update, dedup-merge, prune, distill, discover) covered with NO per-path code. Count and shape violations are hard errors; the subject-word strip is silent.

**Parse is deliberately ASYMMETRIC to serialize.** `ParseFact` DROPS what the gate would reject rather than failing, matching how refs and origin already behave. The reason is durability: a fact version that was legal when committed must stay readable forever, and a tightened rule must never make history unparseable. Do not "fix" this into symmetry.

**Nil vs empty is load-bearing.** The field is emitted only when non-empty and left nil when absent, so the pre-motif corpus round-trips BYTE-IDENTICALLY and an omitted list cannot produce a different blob hash from an empty one. Facts are content-addressed; a cosmetic serialization difference would re-mint every row.

**Why the stemmer moved.** `internal/textnorm` exists because the subject strip and domain matching must share one definition of "the same token". `internal/domain/fact` imports no internal package by design, so it could not reach the copy that lived in `internal/engine/store`.

**Measured on a real 1674-fact corpus:** the subject strip removed 96.1% of motifs built from a fact's own subject words, and the generic control `zero-value-as-valid` survived on 1674 of 1674 — neither inert nor over-broad.
