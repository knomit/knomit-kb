# Revocation reaches established TLS connections by re-judging a ConnState-fed registry at first track, after every adoption, and on a 10s ticker that also adopts — not by sweeping only on adoption, and not by a revocation-only check

Before knomit#258, `reloader.refresh` ran ONLY from a ClientHello (`GetConfigForClient`), and nothing re-checked a connection after its handshake. A revoked peer on a kept-alive connection kept it while it kept talking (the inherited `IdleTimeout` of 60s only reaps idle ones).

OPTIONS WEIGHED at priming (worker notes D1–D5 and the reviewer's B2/B3, ruled by the master session, 2026-09-23):
- Sweep only when a new snapshot is ADOPTED (the original plan). Rejected: a handshake in flight during the adoption, or a connection that handshook and sent nothing until after the adoption's sweep, is tracked AFTER the sweep and would survive until the next adoption, possibly forever.
- Sweep with `IsRevoked(crl, serial)` only. Rejected: a second verifier beside `VerifyInstanceChain` (the two-verifiers-drift motif). The full check also cuts on expiry and on replace-root, which the master ruled is the SAME invariant (would a fresh handshake accept this peer now?).
- Track at the listener (an Accept wrapper). Not needed: ALPN h2 is not negotiated, so ConnState sees every connection. It would also be blocked by the r.TLS wrapper gotcha unless it returned the bare `*tls.Conn`.

CHOSEN: `pki.NewServer(dir, keyPath, logf, interval)` returns a `Server` with `TLSConfig()`, `ConnState` and `Run(ctx)`. The connection is judged at first track, after a handshake-driven adoption, and on every `Run` tick, which also adopts without a handshake. The default is `DefaultRecheckInterval` = 10s, a policy value (not a corpus property), overridable only by tests (`cmd.tlsRecheckInterval`). `Run` stops when its ctx is cancelled. `openTLSServer` starts it on the serve command's context. `pki.ServerConfig` keeps its signature for its test callers and is built on NewServer, without a ticker.

Consequence: `cmd/serve.go` is the only production site. The desktop's `bootServer` has no TLS listener yet (#256); when it gets one it must take the same three pieces.
