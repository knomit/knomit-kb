# go-pluralize chosen as the domain-token singularizer over Porter/Snowball/inflection/golem

DECISION (PR #70): the match-key singularizer for domain tokens (stemDomainToken in internal/store/domain_match.go) is gertd/go-pluralize, NOT a hand-rolled stemmer, Porter/Snowball, jinzhu/inflection, or golem.

OPTIONS CONSIDERED, evaluated against the REAL domain corpus (540 distinct tags / 451 distinct tokens drawn from knomit.db + knomit-kb.db) on two properties the matcher REQUIRES — pair-symmetry (singular(plural)==singular and both collapse to one key) and idempotency (singular(singular(x))==singular(x)):
- Hand-rolled suffix rules: asymmetric on -is/-es and -ss/-sses; not idempotent. Rejected.
- Porter / Snowball: break pair-symmetry (analyses != analysis) AND idempotency. Unusable for a bidirectional match key.
- jinzhu/inflection: passes symmetry+idempotency BUT unmaintained (last release 2019, v1.0.0) and over-singularizes 19 non-plural tokens ending in 's'.
- golem (dictionary lemmatizer): better, but adds ~320K of data, is stale, and still misses repos/analyses.
- go-pluralize (MIT, pure-Go, zero deps, actively maintained port of JS `pluralize` with a real irregular/exception table): symmetric + idempotent on the irregulars (analyses≡analysis, indices≡index, matrices≡matrix, theses≡thesis).

RATIONALE / THE CHOICE: go-pluralize + two guards reduces over-singularization to a SINGLE symmetric, harmless case (https->http) with ZERO pair-symmetry and ZERO idempotency violations across all 451 real tokens. The two guards in stemDomainToken: (1) len<=3 skips acronyms/identifiers (ai, aws, llm, tls) which a pluralizer would mangle via a trailing 's'; (2) a '...ics' suffix guard leaves field/mass nouns singular (economics, robotics, metrics, ethics). BOTH guards are applied IDENTICALLY at index time and query time, so they can never break matching — they only avoid mangling internal keys. The stemmed form is a MATCH-ONLY key: never displayed, never stored as the canonical tag.
