# When git finds a conflict on a fact, knomit MERGES the two versions (as dedup does) and records the merge and its strategy on the commit, instead of picking one side. Human and LLM mergers are future hooks, not built

THE USER'S REFRAME (2026-09-30, verbatim):

"I think the issue is we truly make no attempts to actually merge - we either accept one of the other, but never merge them both and provide evidence of it. The way knomit works, if you remember, is as a temporal graph, and facts can refer to each other. With that in mind, here are the merge scenarios as I see them: 1) auto merge: we already have an example of this, and this is the dedup phase of synthesis. There, synthesis identifies conflicts (duplicates in this case) and takes a stance and merges. Why don't we do the same in this case, meaning, if there is a conflict do something akin to what dedup does - choose a strategy to merge (keep the most confident fact, keep the most trusted agent, whatever), merge the refs is different, domains, motifs, etc (like dedup does), record in the commit message a conflict was merged and with what strategy, then move on 2) human merges: not to implement now, but in that case we surface and someone chooses a merge strategy 3) LLM merges: not to implement now, but when we will have an LLM internal we could use it for merging."

OPTIONS CONSIDERED: (a) accept the winner and only document it (rev 1 of the RCA); (b) make the host's refusal stick; (c) change the peer's own strategy (refuse, or remote-wins); (d) a deterministic fact-level three-way merge at git-merge time, recorded on the commit (rev 2).

CHOICE: (d). This is scenario 1, built as fact.MergeVersions + StrategyMergeFacts, shipped in #355 (merge 68cc310b). Scenarios 2 (a human merges) and 3 (an LLM merges) are future producers of the same Resolution{Body} seam and are NOT built.

RATIONALE: dedup already takes a stance and merges; a git conflict has something dedup lacks, the common ancestor, so most fields need no strategy at all. The merge is a pure function of the three blobs, so the peer and the host compute the same bytes and the pair converges instead of ping-ponging. The two input versions stay in the merge commit's parents as evidence, and the trailer names them.

REJECTED: accept-and-document only; make refusals stick; change the peer's own strategy (refuse / remote-wins); `trusted` and `newest` strategies now; splicing bodies; edit-wins on modify/delete; human and LLM mergers now (future hooks only).

NON-SCOPE: this does not authorize an LLM or text-level merge of title/body (ruling 4), or merging non-fact files. Those keep their site's side-pick, recorded.
