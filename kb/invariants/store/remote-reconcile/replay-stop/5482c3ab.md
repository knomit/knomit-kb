# The rewind replay (replayOntoUpstream) never re-submits a commit the new upstream's history holds: the first-parent walk stops at the watermark OR at the first commit reachable from upstreamTip, whichever comes first; a diverged branch whose walk is empty (only merges beyond upstream) moves onto upstreamTip instead of being pushed as it stood

RULE (branch fix/fleet-switch-replay, verified at b4874ee2; re-pin at the merge commit). On the rewind path (reconcileMain reported ModeRewound, so reconcileAgent routes to reconcileAgentRebase -> replayOntoUpstream), unpushedCommits walks the agent branch's first-parent chain from its tip and stops at EITHER of two points, whichever it meets first: (1) the stop point it was given (the watermark refs/knomit/agent-base/<branch>, or the merge base when there is none), or (2) the first commit that upstreamTip's history holds (one walkHistory of the upstream, skipped when the two histories are disjoint). Merge commits on the way are still skipped. So a commit already upstream is never replayed, whatever the watermark says.

SECOND RULE: when that walk yields NO commits, replayOntoUpstream distinguishes three shapes: local == upstream (no-op); local is an ancestor of upstream (fast-forward); upstream is an ancestor of local (local strictly ahead: no-op, the force-push advances origin). In the remaining shape, the two DIVERGED and everything local beyond the upstream is merges: the replay runs with zero commits and the branch moves onto upstreamTip (ModeRebase, NumReplayed 0). Before this, that shape was taken for 'strictly ahead' and the branch was force-pushed as it stood, re-submitting its merges (and what they brought in) as this copy's own work.

WHY: the watermark can lie arbitrarily far behind. A copy with NO origin (a hub, or any local-only repo) never advances it: only InitRepo/InitFromRemote seed it and only the origin reconcile (reconcileAgentMerge/reconcileAgentRebase) advances it; the local reconcile never touches it. So the first rewind after such a copy attaches an origin used to replay its WHOLE history since InitRepo onto the new main, local side winning every conflict (StrategyLocalWins), re-signed by this copy. That is #528 N1: after a self-hosted master switch onto a new master whose main lagged the old hub's, the old master's create-time host record and a re-enrolled member's first leaf were replayed over the new main's, and the new host's judge refused the push (host-record; artifact-replace leaf-renewal) on every retry.

CONSEQUENCES. (a) A former hub that follows a new host replays only its own non-merge commits made after the new host's last fetch from it; its consensus merges of members' branches are dropped (members push their own branches to the new host). (b) Do not 'fix' a stale watermark by re-seeding it at attach time instead: the walk rule already bounds the replay, and an attach-time seed equal to local main makes the walk empty on a diverged branch, which is exactly the shape the second rule handles.

MISREADINGS. (1) 'The replay now uses the merge base instead of the watermark': no, the watermark still stops the walk when it comes first (G6 scrub semantics on a disjoint rewind are unchanged). (2) 'Nothing of the old master's own is replayed any more': its own commits that the new main lacks ARE replayed, as before; a master-only write among them (made after the new host's last fetch, i.e. two masters at once) is still judged by the new host and can still be refused; the member's status then reports last_error_commit_refused.
