# Review's prune `settle {path, outcome, by}` and distill `settle [{path, outcome}]` write the citing fact(s) and the hypothesis delete as ONE mustExist commit with a Knomit-Outcome trailer; prune refuses a `by` that is merged in the same response, and a distill settle no written fact cites leaves the hypothesis live

PRUNE (internal/corpus/synthesize/settle.go, applyPruneSettle). Every `by` fact gains the hypothesis in its refs and the hypothesis is deleted in ONE BatchWriteFactsMustExist commit (the hypothesis path is mustExist under the branch lock). Refused, writing nothing: an invalid outcome; empty `by`; a hypothesis already removed in this call; a path that is not a live hypothesis; a `by` that is itself a hypothesis (predictions cannot settle predictions); a `by` removed earlier in the call; a `by` whose context is malformed (a rewrite would drop it).

THE MERGE-MEMBER REFUSAL (found in #648 review r1, B1). Prune applies its decisions BEFORE its merges. If `by` is a member of a merge in the same response, settling would cite a fact that the merge then deletes and leave the retracted hypothesis cited by nothing. applyPruneSettle therefore refuses a `by` in mergeMembers with 'by X is merged in this same response ... settle it by the merged fact in a later pass'. The hypothesis stays live and the refusal is reported as a warning.

DISTILL (decision.go, ApplyDistillDecisions). A settled hypothesis leaves in the SAME commit as the synthesized fact that cites it, with the trailer, and is a mustExist path under the lock. A distill settle that NO written synthesized fact cites deletes nothing: one warning ('no written synthesized fact settles it; left live').

DEDUP. dedupCluster's hypothesis loser of a non-hypothesis winner is cited and retracted in one commit with NO trailer and NO field transfer: see kb/gotchas/synthesize/dedup/mechanical-dedup-deletes-distinct-siblings/df4d226d.md.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. A prune settle is not a merge: nothing is folded and no fields move. 'Settle it in a later pass' is the intended recovery, not a retry of the same response. The guard covers the citer being merged away in the same response; it does not cover any other way a citer could disappear.
