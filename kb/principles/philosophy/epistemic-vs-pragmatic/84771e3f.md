# Facts are typed by how they're used: epistemic vs pragmatic

Every fact carries a kind that tells the consumer how to apply it. Epistemic facts describe what is — observations, concepts, syntheses, hypotheses — knowledge to weigh as evidence, with confidence that can rise or fall. Pragmatic facts prescribe what to do — policies and heuristics — rules to follow or rules of thumb to lean on. The leaf type refines this further, but the epistemic/pragmatic split is the primary fork: it is the difference between "this is true" and "do this."

Why: an agent consuming the KB must know whether a fact is a belief it should reason about or an instruction it should obey. Collapsing the two leads to agents treating tentative observations as mandates, or ignoring hard policies as mere opinions. Typing by use makes the corpus actionable — recall can surface the right kind for the task, and confidence means different things for each (evidence strength vs. how firmly the rule is held).

What NOT to do:
- Write a policy as if it were an observation, or vice versa, to dodge the type's constraints
- Bury a prescriptive "always do X" inside an epistemic fact's body
- Ignore kind when consuming facts — an agent should apply an observation and a policy differently
