# Every fact declares what it is about

A fact is never just free text. Each one declares its subject along three independent axes: an ontology path (topic/category/...) that places it in a navigable tree, domain tags that cut across that tree, and named entities it concerns. Together these make the corpus introspectable — an agent can ask not only "what do I know about X?" but "what is the shape of everything I know?" and get a structured answer.

Why: knowledge you cannot navigate is knowledge you cannot trust you've fully consulted. The ontology gives a human or agent a map; domains and entities give orthogonal lenses for the same facts. Without self-description, the KB collapses into a flat bag of text searchable only by embedding luck, with no way to enumerate coverage or spot gaps.

What NOT to do:
- Park facts under a flat or arbitrary category just to get them written ("misc", "notes")
- Encode subject information in the body that should be a domain tag or entity
- Treat the ontology path as decorative — it is a query and navigation surface, not a filename
